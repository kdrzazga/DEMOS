1

1928-39

The story of Jack Tramiel begins in Poland, in the city of Woo-g. He was born on December 13th, 1928 in a Jewish family as Yatseck or Eedek Tsmeel.

He was the son of Abram Yosef Tsmeel and Rifka Bentkovskah. 

The whole family lived on Nah-pure-kovski-ehgo Street in Goorniahk district in Woo-g. Today this street in named pshee-beeshevskiehgoh.

2

1939-44

During World War 2, Poland, including Woo-g was occupied by Nazi Germany.
The city was renamed to Litzmannstadt.
Tramiel's family was transported to the Jewish ghetto, where Jack worked in a garment factory.

3.

1944-45
 In August 1944, the Ghetto was liquidated and Jack was transported to Auschwitz - the infamous German concentration camp.
He was examined by Joseph Mengele (a.k.a. Angel of Death) himself and selected for a work party.

4.

1945
 Later he and his father were sent to the labor camp Ahlem, near Hanover.

Tramiel was rescued from the camp in April 1945 by the 84th Infantry Division of the U.S. Army

5.

1947
Tramiel married Helen Goldgrub
On November 10, 1947 they immigrated to the United States.
Jack soon joined the U.S. Army, where he learned how to repair office equipment, including typewriters.

6.
1953
Tramiel was working as a taxi driver in New York. He used his Army benefits and a loan to buy a shop in the Bronx to repair office machinery.
 His company was named COMMODORE PORTABLE TYPEWRITER, as Tramiel liked military-style names.

7.
1956

Three years later Tramiel signed a deal with Czechoslovak typewriter manufacturer Zbrojovka Brno NP to assemble and sell their typewriters in North America.
 However, as Czechoslovakia was part of the Warsaw Pact, they could not be imported directly into the USA.
As a walkaround, Commodore Business Machines company was established in Toronto, Canada
