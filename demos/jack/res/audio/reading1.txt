1

1928-39

The story of Jack Tramiel begins in Poland, in the city of Woo-g. He was born on December 13th, 1928 in a Jewish family as Yatseck or Eedek Tsmeel.

He was the son of Abram Yosef Tsmeel and Rifka Bentkovskah. 

The whole family lived on Nah-pure-kovski-ehgo Street in Goorniahk district in Woo-g. Today this street in named pshee-beeshevskiehgoh.

2

1939-44

During World War 2, Poland, including Woo-g was occupied by Nazi Germany.
The city was renamed to Litzmannstadt.
Tramiel's family was transported to the Jewish ghetto, where Jack worked in a garment factory.

3.

1944-45
 In August 1944, the Ghetto was liquidated and Jack was transported to Auschwitz - the infamous German concentration camp.
He was examined by Joseph Mengele (a.k.a. Angel of Death) himself and selected for a work party.

4.

1945
 Later he and his father were sent to the labor camp Ahlem, near Hanover.

Tramiel was rescued from the camp in April 1945 by the 84th Infantry Division of the U.S. Army

5.

1947
Tramiel married Helen Goldgrub
On November 10, 1947 they immigrated to the United States.
Jack soon joined the U.S. Army, where he learned how to repair office equipment, including typewriters.

6.
1953
Tramiel was working as a taxi driver in New York. He used his Army benefits and a loan to buy a shop in the Bronx to repair office machinery.
 His company was named COMMODORE PORTABLE TYPEWRITER, as Tramiel liked military-style names.

7.
1956

Three years later Tramiel signed a deal with Czechoslovak typewriter manufacturer Zbrojovka Brno NP to assemble and sell their typewriters in North America.
 However, as Czechoslovakia was part of the Warsaw Pact, they could not be imported directly into the USA.
As a walkaround, Commodore Business Machines company was established in Toronto, Canada

8.
1962

Six years laterCommodore began selling portable typewriters made with parts from older Rheinmetall-Borsig machines from Germany.
The company went public.

9.
Tramiel travelled to Japan, when he saw an electronic calculator and understood that typewriters were a dead end.

10.
1971
Commodore started to produce its own calculators, initially with Texas Instrument chips, but then the company acquired MOS Technology company and started using their own chips.
Chuck Peddle, the lead designer from MOS told Tramiel that calculators would soon be replaced with personal computers. Tramiel told him to build one.

11.
1977
And Peddle did so! Commodore unveiled the PET, built on its own MOS 6502 chip. Dealer demand was immediate, and it sold well in education and Europe.

12.
Early 1980s
To compete with colour machines like the Apple II and Atari 8-bits, Commodore launched the VIC-20, the first computer to sell a million units, and then the Commodore 64, the best-selling home computer ever.
In 1981 Adam Osborne (a British-American computer author and publisher) described Tramiel as ruthless but credited his results.

13.
1983
Commodore’s aggressive pricing helped push Texas Instruments out of home computers. Commodore’s revenue passed $1 billion, up from under $100 million three years earlier.

14.
January 1984:
 At Consumer Electronics Show in Chicago, Tramiel announced the billion-dollar year. On January 13 he walked out of a board meeting with Gould, and he resigned days later. Commodore Magazine was then forbidden to mention him.

15:
1984: Jack founded Tramel Technology, spelled that way so people would say “tra-MEL.” In July it bought Atari’s consumer division from Warner and became Atari Corporation.

16:
Mid-1980s

Atari released the ST, a 68000-based rival to the Mac and Amiga. It succeeded in Europe and among musicians, but many retailers and software firms distrusted Tramiel.

17:
1986

In a German interview he explained his exit from Commodore as disagreements with Commodore chairman Irving Gould  over spending and over raising capital through new shares.

18:
Late 1980s:
Jack Tramiel stepped back from daily operations and made his son Sam CEO of Atari.

19:
1993:
When The US Holocaust Memorial Museum was opened in Washington DC, Jack Tramiel, an Auschwitz survivor, was a co-founder.

1995:
After a few years Sam Tramiel had a heart attack and Jack returned to run Atari.

1996:
However, a year later, he sold Atari to JTS (hard-disk-drive producer) in a reverse merger, joined the JTS board, and retired to Monte Sereno, California.

1998:
During an interview to FORTUNE magazine, Jack told his famous sentence: Business i  war and he believed in winning, not compromise.

2004:
As a retired person, Jack  made his last visit to his hometown, Woo-j.

2012:
Several years later, He died of heart failure on April 8, 2012, at the age of 83.
